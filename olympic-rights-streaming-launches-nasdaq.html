Olympic Rights, Streaming Launches, and a NASDAQ Listing: The Strategic Moments That Defined David Leavy's Tenure at Discovery

📅 October 5, 2026
✎ David Leavy
⌚ 2 min read

Over more than twenty years at Discovery Inc., David Leavy was involved in four decisions that marked distinct phases of the company's growth: a NASDAQ listing, a pan-European Olympic rights agreement, a major acquisition and a streaming launch.

Each represented a different kind of challenge: capital markets, international sports rights, corporate integration and direct-to-consumer technology. Taken in sequence, they trace how Discovery changed, and how the role of David C. Leavy, a Washington, D.C.-based executive who would later become Chief Corporate Operations Officer, widened with it.

2008: The NASDAQ listing

In 2008, Discovery Communications began trading on the NASDAQ exchange, giving the company a public-market identity of its own. Leavy supported the listing, which set the company's footing as a public company for the decade that followed.

Listings require coordinated communication with investors, regulators, employees and the press, all held to the precision that disclosure rules demand. It is the kind of work in which a communications discipline formed in government translates directly.

Taken in sequence, the four moments trace how Discovery changed, and how his role widened with it.

Olympic rights across Europe

Discovery and Eurosport's agreement for the rights to the Olympic Games across Europe added an international dimension to the company and to Leavy's own record. The arrangement placed a U.S.-based media company at the center of how audiences across much of the continent would follow the Games.

A rights agreement on that scale involves sporting bodies, national broadcasters, regulators and audiences in many markets at once. Managing those relationships calls for the cross-border, cross-functional coordination that runs through his career, including his later operating work at CNN.

Scripps and discovery+

In 2018, Discovery completed its acquisition of Scripps Networks Interactive, a deal valued at about $14.6 billion that brought HGTV, Food Network and Travel Channel into the company. Leavy supported the agreement, which substantially expanded Discovery's U.S. portfolio of lifestyle and factual networks.

That expanded library fed into the January 2021 launch of discovery+, where Leavy was a central figure. Bringing a streaming service to market required content, technology and commercial teams to work to one plan and one launch date, a test of coordination as much as of product.

Conclusion

None of these moments stands alone. Together they describe an executive who moved between capital markets, international rights, mergers and product launches without changing his basic method.
